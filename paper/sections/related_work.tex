\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{DID identification and its threats.}
A large recent econometrics literature has formalized when DID estimators fail.
Goodman-Bacon \cite{goodmanbacon2021} decomposes the two-way fixed-effects
estimator under variation in treatment timing and exposes the ``forbidden''
comparisons it makes between newly and already-treated units; de Chaisemartin and
D'Haultf{\oe}uille \cite{dechaisemartin2020} show such estimators can place
negative weights on treatment effects; and Callaway and Sant'Anna
\cite{callaway2021} and Sun and Abraham \cite{sunabraham2021} propose estimators
robust to heterogeneous, staggered effects. Roth et al. \cite{roth2023} survey
the design's assumptions and the subtleties of pre-trend testing. ARGUS does not
add an estimator; it \emph{operationalizes} this literature as an auditable
rubric, turning each known threat into a dimension whose supporting evidence can
be checked in a given paper (\S\ref{sec:framework}).

\paragraph{Responsible AI for empirical research.}
A growing body of work applies AI to research reliability---automated
reproducibility checking, statistical-reporting validation, and AI-assisted peer
review. ARGUS is complementary but differs in target and in epistemic posture.
First, it audits \emph{identification credibility} specifically, the part of an
empirical argument that determines whether a causal claim is licensed at all,
rather than surface statistical reporting. Second, and centrally, it does not
issue verdicts: it produces evidence-anchored, human-reviewable reports that
localize risk, leaving adjudication to a domain expert. This human-in-the-loop,
``flag-not-judge'' stance is a deliberate response to the fact that the
counterfactual is unobserved and that automated causal verdicts would be both
unreliable and unaccountable.

\paragraph{Benchmark and evaluation methodology.}
Our evaluation design follows three lessons from the benchmarking literature.
Holistic evaluation~\cite{liang2023helm} argues against collapsing capability to a
single accuracy number; ARGUS likewise reports a layered profile---retrieval
quality, relevance, adequacy, and localized risk---rather than one detection rate.
Dynamic, adversarial benchmarking~\cite{kiela2021dynabench} warns that static
benchmarks saturate; our flaw-injection loop is an adversarial, model-in-the-loop
construction that we intend to escalate as the assessor improves (our synthetic
flaws already saturate, motivating harder ones). Finally, Raji et
al.~\cite{raji2021benchmark} caution that turning an abstract capability into a
measurable task is a consequential, non-neutral move; we take this seriously in
\S\ref{sec:discussion}, where we state precisely what ARGUS does and does not
measure. Should the annotated corpus be released, we will document it with a
datasheet~\cite{gebru2021datasheets}.

\paragraph{Execution-grounded verification vs.\ assumption auditing.}
A complementary line asks whether a model can \emph{carry out} causal inference
correctly. CausalVerify~\cite{causalverify}, an execution-grounded benchmark,
runs a model's generated estimation code against synthetic data and checks that
the recovered effect matches the canonical estimator on the realised dataset.
ARGUS audits a different layer: not whether the estimation is \emph{executed}
correctly, but whether the identification \emph{assumptions} behind it are
adequately supported by the paper's reported evidence---which CausalVerify
explicitly places outside its scope (``identification judgment \dots\ and
assumption checking \dots\ outside the direct scope''). ARGUS builds on the
corpus of published economics papers released with that
benchmark~\cite{causalverify_dataset}, adding a new dimension-level
identification-risk annotation layer on top of it.

\paragraph{Bounded LLM agents.}
Tool-using agents in the ReAct style interleave reasoning and acting over an open
horizon \cite{yao2023react}. ARGUS deliberately \emph{bounds} this paradigm.
Agency is confined to two scoped sub-tasks (extraction and assessment), each run
as a fixed-tool, hard-step-budget loop with a logged trace, while the
inter-module control flow is ordinary deterministic code. We trade autonomy for
two properties the framework depends on: \emph{reproducibility}, so that the
flaw-injection metrics are stable and comparable across runs, and
\emph{evidence-traceability}, so that every flag a human reviews is anchored to
inspectable evidence. We view this restraint as a feature for responsible
auditing rather than a limitation.
