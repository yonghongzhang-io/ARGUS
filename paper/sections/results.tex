\section{Preliminary Results}
\label{sec:results}

As a lower bound and a sanity check on the evaluation methodology, we implement a
deterministic, non-LLM baseline: extraction retrieves evidence by token overlap,
and assessment scores each dimension from the presence or absence of supporting
and risk-indicating vocabulary. We run it over all eleven flaws on two
parsed-paper fixtures---a generic supported DID study and a China carbon-ETS
pilot fixture---injecting one flaw per run. Table~\ref{tab:baseline} reports the
headline metrics; the full per-flaw breakdown is in
\texttt{experiments/phase1\_pilot/results.md}, and Figure~\ref{fig:results} is
generated directly from the live evaluation.

\paragraph{What the baseline gets right.}
The baseline never raises a false alarm: on the clean papers it stays quiet on
every dimension (false-alarm rate $0.000$). It also correctly flags the two
omission flaws---\texttt{measurement\_break} and
\texttt{spillover\_contamination}---whose vocabulary does not appear elsewhere in
the paper, detecting them by the \emph{absence} of evidence. This is exactly the
behaviour we want from a lower bound and confirms the evaluation harness is
measuring something real.

\paragraph{Where it fails, and why that matters.}
Beyond those two cases, detection is low ($0.182$ and $0.091$). Two failure modes
explain it (Figure~\ref{fig:results}, panel~B). First, \emph{cross-section
vocabulary bleed}: deleting the placebo section does not help when another
section also mentions ``placebo'', so retrieval still returns a hit and the
baseline judges the dimension supported. Second, and more fundamentally, the
baseline is blind to \emph{every} commission flaw, where flawed-but-plausible
evidence is present and uses the expected topic vocabulary. The lesson is sharp:
keyword presence is not evidence \emph{adequacy}. Distinguishing a sound
parallel-trends argument from a superficially similar but flawed one requires
reasoning about what the evidence actually shows, not whether the right words
appear. This is precisely the gap a reasoning-based auditor must close, and it
frames the doctoral programme that follows (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).
