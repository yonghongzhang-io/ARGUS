\section{Discussion and Research Plan}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
The present results are deliberately preliminary. They use a small number of
synthetic fixtures rather than a real corpus, and the baseline is an intentional
lower bound, so its low detection rate should be read as a calibrated starting
point, not as evidence about the eventual system. More fundamentally, flaw
injection certifies an auditor against the \emph{injected} threat on a single
dimension; it does not certify the absence of other, un-injected threats, and it
inherits whatever blind spots the flaw taxonomy has. We therefore treat detection
and localization as necessary, not sufficient, evidence of an auditor's quality,
and pair them with the false-alarm rate to guard against a system that flags
everything.

\paragraph{Research plan.}
The doctoral programme is organized around closing the reasoning gap that
\S\ref{sec:results} makes concrete, in four stages.
\emph{(1)~Reasoning-based extraction and assessment.} Replace the keyword policy
inside the bounded loop with a model that judges evidence \emph{adequacy}---does
the reported event study actually support parallel trends?---evaluated against the
baseline under the same flaw-injection protocol, so that any gain is attributable
and reproducible.
\emph{(2)~A real annotated corpus.} Build a corpus of published DID
environmental-policy studies with expert annotations of identification risk per
dimension, moving evaluation beyond synthetic fixtures and enabling agreement
studies against human auditors.
\emph{(3)~Human-expert studies.} Measure whether ARGUS reports actually help
domain experts---calibration of trust, time saved, and errors avoided---since the
system is explicitly designed to assist, not replace, expert judgement.
\emph{(4)~Broader policy domains.} Extend beyond the China emissions-trading case
to other environmental and, eventually, general empirical-economics settings,
testing how far the rubric-plus-injection methodology generalizes.
