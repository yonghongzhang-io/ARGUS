\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have argued that the right target for AI assistance in empirical policy
research is not the unobservable causal effect but the \emph{identification
credibility} of the study that estimates it. ARGUS operationalizes this stance: a
bounded-agentic pipeline decomposes identification into auditable dimensions,
gathers and assesses in-paper evidence, and localizes risk in a transparent,
human-reviewable report, while confining model agency to two scoped, traceable
sub-tasks. We validate such auditors with synthetic flaw injection, which
supplies reproducible local ground truth without access to the true effect. Once
the leakage that made this evaluation circular is removed, a keyword baseline is
blind to flawed-but-plausible evidence ($0.182$ detection), while a model that
judges evidence adequacy recovers it ($1.000$, no false alarms). Presence, again,
is not adequacy. Scaling this from synthetic fixtures to a real, expert-annotated
corpus with humans in the loop is the doctoral programme. Auditing identification
credibility is, we argue, both a tractable and a responsible role for AI in
empirical environmental-policy research.
