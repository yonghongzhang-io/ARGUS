\begin{abstract}
Empirical economics leans heavily on difference-in-differences (DID) designs---%
nowhere more visibly than in environmental policy, our motivating case---whose
credibility rests on identification assumptions that are often asserted rather
than audited. We propose \textbf{ARGUS}, a responsible-AI framework that audits
the \emph{identification credibility} of such studies rather than adjudicating
the true causal effect, which is never observed. ARGUS decomposes identification
into auditable dimensions, gathers in-paper evidence, assesses each
assumption--implication--evidence chain, and localizes risk in a transparent,
human-reviewable report. We validate the auditor with \emph{synthetic flaw
injection}, which provides reproducible local ground truth without the true
effect. Once the leakage that makes naive evaluation circular is removed, a
deterministic keyword baseline detects only $0.182$ of injected flaws and is blind
to flawed-but-present evidence; swapping only the assessment policy to a model that
judges evidence \emph{adequacy} recovers detection to $1.000$ with no false alarms,
showing that evidence presence is not evidence adequacy. On 27 real published DID
papers the binding constraint shifts to \emph{evidence grounding}: the framework
honestly reports retrieval failure rather than over-flagging, localizing where
real-paper auditing is hard.
\keywords{Responsible AI \and Causal inference \and Difference-in-differences
\and Environmental policy \and LLM agents.}
\end{abstract}
