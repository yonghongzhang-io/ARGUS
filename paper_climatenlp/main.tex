\begin{abstract}
Difference-in-differences (DID) studies are widely used to evaluate climate
policy, yet their identification assumptions are more often \emph{asserted} than
\emph{audited}. Because the counterfactual is unobserved, we ask instead whether
each assumption is adequately supported by the evidence a paper reports. We
introduce \sys{}, an end-to-end, \emph{bounded-agentic} auditing system in which
an LLM runs behind a fixed, deterministic pipeline and scores DID studies on an
11-dimension assumption--implication--evidence rubric, retrieving evidence,
judging its adequacy, and producing a risk report rather than a causal
verdict. Under realistic flaw injection (seeding one known error into an
otherwise credible study) adequacy reasoning detects flaws that keyword matching
cannot ($0.18$ versus up to $1.00$ detection, all at zero false alarms), and a
compute-graded ablation attributes the gain to per-dimension decomposition, with
the retrieval gate trading detection for calibrated abstention. Across 27 real
papers evidence grounding emerges as the coverage bottleneck, and a pilot
diagnostic study reveals a systematic over-severity that a lightweight
calibration layer partly corrects. \sys{} is
thus a high-sensitivity screening tool that surfaces weakly evidenced
identification assumptions for expert review.
\end{abstract}
