\begin{abstract}
Difference-in-differences (DID) studies are widely used to evaluate climate
policy, yet their identification assumptions are more often \emph{asserted} than
\emph{audited}. Because the counterfactual is unobserved, we ask instead whether
each assumption is adequately supported by the evidence a paper reports. We
introduce \sys{}, an end-to-end, \emph{bounded-agentic} auditing system in which
an LLM runs behind a fixed, deterministic pipeline and scores DID studies on an
11-dimension assumption--implication--evidence rubric, retrieving evidence,
judging its adequacy, and producing a risk report for expert review rather than
adjudicating causal truth. Under realistic flaw injection \sys{} catches all 11
injected flaws versus 5 for a same-model single-pass baseline, with no false
alarms; across 27 real papers it exposes evidence grounding as the bottleneck,
abstaining when support is absent; and a pilot expert study reveals a systematic
over-severity that a lightweight calibration layer partly corrects. \sys{} is
thus a high-sensitivity screening tool that surfaces weakly evidenced
identification assumptions for expert review.
\end{abstract}
