\begin{abstract}
Difference-in-differences (DID) studies are widely used to evaluate climate
policy, but assessing the evidence supporting their identification assumptions
remains challenging. We introduce \sys{}, a structured language-model pipeline
that audits reported evidence against an eleven-dimension
assumption--implication--evidence rubric and abstains when relevant evidence
cannot be retrieved. We evaluate \sys{} using injected flaws, published
economics papers, and a small expert-annotated pilot. On the 11-flaw benchmark,
\sys{} detects $73\%$ of planted flaws, compared with $18\%$ for a keyword-based
pipeline. On real papers, \sys{} abstains on about $40\%$ of dimensions for lack
of retrievable evidence and, where it answers, is more severe than the experts
on 28 of 33 labels. A simple post-hoc calibration rule reduces over-severity on
the same pilot used to develop it. \sys{} provides evidence-linked risk reports
that localize potential weaknesses for expert review, without adjudicating
causal claims. Code and data:
\url{https://github.com/yonghongzhang-io/ARGUS}.
\end{abstract}
