\begin{abstract}
Climate policy leans on ex-post causal evaluations---difference-in-differences
(DID) studies of carbon pricing, emissions-trading pilots, and emission
standards---whose credibility rests on identification assumptions that are
often \emph{asserted} rather than \emph{audited}. Because the counterfactual is
never observed, asking whether an estimated effect is \emph{true} is the wrong
target; we ask instead whether each identifying assumption is supported by the
evidence the paper reports, and where that support is weakest. We present
\sys{}, a responsible-AI framework that decomposes DID identification into an
11-dimension rubric of assumption--implication--evidence chains, retrieves
dimension-specific evidence, assesses reported-evidence adequacy with a
\emph{bounded-agentic} audit module, and localizes risk in a human-reviewable
report---flagging dimensions for expert review, not adjudicating causal truth.
We validate the auditor with \emph{flaw injection} as local ground truth (no
access to the true effect): adequacy reasoning catches flawed-but-present
evidence that keyword presence cannot, raising synthetic flaw detection from
\NUM{0.455} to \NUM{1.000} over a same-model single-pass baseline, which on 27
real DID papers preserves an explicit \texttt{unknown} state
(\NUM{38.9\%} vs \NUM{0.4\%}) that keeps evidence-grounding uncertainty visible.
A pilot expert gold (5 papers, 55 cells) reveals a one-directional
over-severity bias that an applicability-aware, anchor-based calibration layer
reduces while preserving high-risk recall. \sys{} is best read as a
high-sensitivity screening tool for the credibility of climate-policy evidence.
\end{abstract}
