\begin{abstract}
Difference-in-differences (DID) studies are widely used to evaluate climate
policy, yet their identification assumptions are often \emph{asserted} rather
than systematically \emph{audited}. Because the true counterfactual is
unobserved, we ask a more tractable question: whether each identifying
assumption is adequately supported by the evidence reported in the paper.

We introduce \sys{}, an end-to-end, \emph{bounded-agentic} auditing
system---an LLM behind a fixed, deterministic pipeline---that audits DID studies
through an 11-dimension assumption--implication--evidence rubric. Its pipeline
retrieves relevant evidence, assesses its adequacy, and produces an
evidence-grounded risk report for expert review rather than judging causal
truth.

Using realistic flaw injection, \sys{} detects all 11 injected identification
flaws, compared with 5 under a same-model single-pass baseline, with no false
alarms on clean cases. On 27 real papers, it exposes evidence grounding as the
main bottleneck by explicitly abstaining when relevant support cannot be found.
A pilot expert study further reveals systematic over-severity, which a
lightweight calibration layer partially reduces. \sys{} is therefore best viewed
as a high-sensitivity screening tool for identifying weakly supported causal
claims.
\end{abstract}
