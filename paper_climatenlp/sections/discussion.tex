\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What \sys{} measures.} A measurement is only as good as the link
between the construct and the task that operationalizes
it~\cite{jacobs2021measurement,raji2021benchmark}. \sys{} audits whether a
climate-policy study's identification assumptions are \emph{adequately supported
by its reported evidence}: reported-evidence adequacy is an operational proxy for
identification credibility, not causal validity itself. The two can diverge, so
\sys{} surfaces risk for an expert to weigh and makes no ruling on the
effect itself.

\paragraph{Why climate policy.} The identification challenges above stress
several assumptions at once, and climate-policy estimates can inform
governance and resource allocation, so a confidently wrong credibility
judgement has a cost; \sys{} therefore abstains when its gate finds no relevant passage.

\paragraph{Retrieval limits audit coverage.} A further gap separates
the construct from the task: retrieving the relevant evidence. On the
real-paper corpus it limits coverage, and in the pilot an assessor handed
weakly relevant evidence usually rates the dimension high (20 of 22
weak-retrieval pilot cells), so \sys{} reports a
\emph{retrieval-quality} status and an explicit \texttt{unknown} risk distinct
from a substantive high-risk judgement. This is why the pipeline separates
retrieval, relevance filtering, and adequacy assessment.
