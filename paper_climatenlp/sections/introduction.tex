\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Difference-in-differences (DID) is a workhorse design for evaluating climate and
environmental policy: studies of carbon pricing, emissions-trading pilots, and
emission standards compare treated and untreated units before and after a policy
to recover its effect. The credibility of any such estimate rests on a chain of
\emph{identification assumptions} (parallel trends, no anticipation, appropriate
handling of staggered adoption, no interference between units), each of which a
decade of methodological work has shown to be fragile and which a standard
two-way fixed-effects specification can silently violate under heterogeneous,
staggered treatment timing
\cite{bertrand2004trust,goodmanbacon2021,callaway2021,sunabraham2021,dechaisemartin2020,roth2023};
even the pre-trends tests used to defend these designs are themselves unreliable
\cite{roth2022}. These difficulties are sharpened in the climate-policy setting,
where staggered rollouts, spatial spillovers, overlapping regulations,
anticipatory abatement, and noisy emissions measurement stress several assumptions
at once (Appendix~\ref{app:climate}).

Yet in practice these assumptions are more often \emph{asserted} than
\emph{audited}: a paper states that parallel trends ``hold'', shows an event-study
plot, and moves on. Checking whether the identification argument is actually
supported by the reported evidence is slow, expert-intensive, and inconsistent
across reviewers, one facet of a broader reliability gap in empirical research
where undisclosed analytic flexibility and specification search move published
conclusions
\cite{leamer1983,ioannidis2005,simmons2011,gelmanloken2014,silberzahn2018many,brodeur2020methods,botviniknezer2020variability}.
Large language models can scale such checks
\cite{zheng2023judge,liang2024feedback,lu2024aiscientist}, but they reproduce
causal \emph{language} more readily than causal \emph{inference}
\cite{kiciman2023causal,zecevic2023causalparrots,jin2024corr2cause}, and existing
benchmarks probe causal reasoning on curated tasks rather than the identification
credibility of real published designs
\cite{jin2023cladder,zhou2024causalbench,lee2025econcausal,sawarni2026causalreasoning,yang2024criticalreview}.
A system that issued causal verdicts would thus be both unreliable and
unaccountable.

\paragraph{Our approach.}
We argue that identification \emph{credibility} is auditable even when the causal
\emph{effect} is not, extending the research-design tradition in applied
econometrics and its move to treat assumptions like parallel trends as a matter of
degree rather than a binary
\cite{angristpischke2010,atheyimbens2017,rambachanroth2023}. Because the
counterfactual is never observed, we ask not whether an estimate is true but
whether each identifying assumption is adequately supported by the evidence the
paper reports, and where that support is weakest. We introduce \sys{}, a
\emph{bounded-agentic} auditor: model agency is confined to two scoped, traceable
sub-tasks behind a fixed, deterministic pipeline that decomposes a DID study into
an 11-dimension rubric, retrieves dimension-specific evidence, judges its adequacy,
and localizes risk in a transparent, evidence-anchored report
\cite{gebru2021datasheets} (Figure~\ref{fig:overview}); unlike free-roaming agents
whose control flow is model-decided \cite{yao2023react}, this keeps the audit
traceable and reproducible. \sys{} flags dimensions for review; it does not
adjudicate causal truth. Lacking the true effect as ground truth, we validate the
auditor by \emph{flaw injection}, perturbing a credible paper with one known flaw
and checking that \sys{} catches it, stays quiet on the clean version, and
localizes the right dimension, in the spirit of adversarial and holistic model
evaluation \cite{kiela2021dynabench,liang2023helm}; a pilot expert gold then
calibrates severity, since credibility is a graded, partly design-dependent
judgement rather than a binary label \cite{jacobs2021measurement,raji2021benchmark}.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(i)}~an \textbf{11-dimension audit framework} for DID identification
credibility whose unit of audit is an explicit
\emph{assumption--implication--evidence} chain, targeting reported-evidence
adequacy rather than causal truth (\S\ref{sec:framework});
\textbf{(ii)}~a \textbf{bounded-agentic} evidence-retrieval and assessment
pipeline, with model agency confined to two scoped, traceable sub-tasks behind a
fixed deterministic control flow;
\textbf{(iii)}~a \textbf{multi-stage evaluation} built on \emph{flaw injection as
local ground truth}, showing that adequacy reasoning catches flawed-but-present
evidence keyword matching cannot, and a real-paper deployment (27 DID papers) that
relocates the bottleneck to \emph{evidence grounding}; an architecture ablation
against a same-model single-pass baseline isolates the bounded design as the
source of the gain (\S\ref{sec:results}); and
\textbf{(iv)}~a \textbf{pilot expert-gold calibration loop} (5 papers, 55
dimension-level cells) that exposes a one-directional over-severity bias and a
lightweight, applicability-aware calibration layer that reduces it while
preserving high-risk recall.
