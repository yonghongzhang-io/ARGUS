\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The right target for AI assistance in empirical climate-policy research is not the
unobservable causal effect but the \emph{identification credibility} of the study
that estimates it. \sys{} operationalizes this stance: a bounded pipeline
decomposes identification into auditable dimensions and localizes risk in an
evidence-grounded, human-reviewable report. Synthetic flaw injection supplies
reproducible local ground truth without access to the true effect: a keyword
baseline stays blind to flawed-but-plausible evidence that an adequacy-judging
model recovers. A
human-gold diagnostic pilot further shows that expert labels can do more than
evaluate the auditor: they expose, and help correct, a systematic over-severity
bias.
Future work: expert-authored flaws, expert annotation at scale (the central
open challenge), studies of whether reports help reviewers, and settings beyond
the China fixture. Auditing identification credibility is, we argue, a
tractable and responsible role for AI in empirical environmental-policy
research.
