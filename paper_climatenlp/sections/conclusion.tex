\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The right target for AI assistance in empirical climate-policy research is not the
unobservable causal effect but the \emph{identification credibility} of the study
that estimates it. \sys{} operationalizes this stance: a bounded-agentic pipeline
decomposes identification into auditable dimensions, gathers and assesses in-paper
evidence, and localizes risk in a transparent, human-reviewable report, while
confining model agency to two scoped, traceable sub-tasks. We evaluate such
auditors with synthetic flaw injection, which supplies reproducible local ground
truth without access to the true effect: once its circular leakage is removed, a
keyword baseline stays blind to flawed-but-plausible evidence ($0.182$ detection)
that an adequacy-judging model recovers ($1.000$, no false alarms). A human-gold diagnostic pilot further shows that expert
labels can do more than evaluate the auditor: they expose a systematic
over-severity bias and calibrate the mapping from evidence gaps to audit risk.
Future work follows directly: hardening the assessor on subtler flaws and across
models under the same injection protocol; scaling to dimension-level expert
annotation with humans in the loop, the central open challenge; human studies of
whether \sys{} reports help reviewers in practice; and extending beyond the China
emissions-trading fixture to broader environmental and empirical-economics
settings. Auditing identification credibility is, we argue, both a tractable and
a responsible role for AI in empirical environmental-policy research.
